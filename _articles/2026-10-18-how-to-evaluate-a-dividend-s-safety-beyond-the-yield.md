A high yield is a price signal before it is an income promise. A holding priced near $50 paying a dollar a quarter advertises eight percent; the identical payment on a share that slid from $50 to $25 advertises sixteen, and screeners treat those two situations as one. Dividend divided by share price rises when either the payment
grows or the price collapses, and screening tools sort by that fraction without asking which happened -- so the top of any
yield-sorted list concentrates companies investors expect to cut. Evaluating safety means ignoring the headline entirely and
examining capacity instead, which is slower work and considerably less exciting [-- payouts themselves get their own
explanation](/articles/what-is-a-dividend-getting-paid-to-own-stocks/).

## Capacity comes from cash, not profit

Accounting earnings include non-cash estimates and exclude reinvestment needs, so a comfortable payout ratio computed against
profit can conceal a company borrowing to pay holders. The better comparison sets distributions against cash generated by
operations after the spending required simply to stay in business. A firm distributing most of that figure runs little margin
for a bad year; one distributing a modest fraction has genuine flexibility even while reporting mediocre margins. Where does the
rest go -- growth investment that produces returns, buybacks at sensible prices, debt reduction, or sitting idle earning little?
The destination matters as much as the amount, since hoarded cash eventually invites pressure to distribute it regardless of
whether operations need it.

Sector norms make identical numbers mean different things. Utilities and infrastructure run high ratios legitimately because
regulated revenue is comparatively predictable and capital programmes are long-planned. Real-estate vehicles must distribute most
taxable income to keep their structure, so judging them on manufacturing-company thresholds produces nonsense. Cyclical producers
look magnificent at cycle peaks, paying generously out of profits that were never normal, then cutting violently when prices
normalise -- which is why peak-cycle cover tells you nothing except that the peak occurred. Banks depend on retained earnings for
regulatory capital ratios, meaning large distributions constrain lending capacity in ways visible only if you understand what the
capital supports [-- leverage gets examined properly elsewhere](/articles/debt-to-equity-measuring-financial-health/).

## Warning signs worth memorising

**Debt rising while dividends climb.** Borrowing to fund distributions converts an income promise into refinancing risk and shows
in filings plainly: interest obligations increasing across several periods alongside flat or falling operating cash flow, dividend
spending exceeding generation. Management teams occasionally do this to postpone a reckoning because announcement-day reaction to a
cut is brutal; understanding that incentive explains behaviour more reliably than press releases do.

**Cut leads deterioration more often than lag follows it.** Boards prefer eliminating payments late and reluctantly, so a
reduction frequently signals conditions already serious rather than merely worrying. That makes reductions valuable information
rather than automatic sell triggers -- sometimes a reset restores health, as when a company traded away a crushing commitment to
fund survival -- but the default reading should be investigative rather than forgiving.

**Streak pride substituting for policy.** Long unbroken records of increases create reputational hostage situations: management
would rather borrow than break a celebrated sequence, and the record becomes marketing independent of underlying economics
[-- growth histories deserve suspicion about how they're constructed](/articles/dividend-aristocrats-25-years-of-rising-payments/).
Ask whether the board's stated framework ties payouts to generation or to maintaining appearances.

**Analyst attention concentration.** Heavily covered dividend names have their weaknesses priced already, whereas neglected
companies can offer genuine surprises in both directions. Low coverage is opportunity only if you do the reading others skip, and
is hazard if you don't.

## Structural alternatives worth comparing

Individual dividend stocks concentrate three hazards simultaneously: company-specific failure, payout policy changes, and the
behavioural temptation to overpay for perceived certainty. Pooled ownership addresses the first two at the cost of giving up
selectivity, and specific styles emphasise consistency of increases rather than absolute level, which historically reduced
volatility relative to broader indexes during downturns -- though such comparisons depend heavily on the era measured and on
whether totals include the payouts themselves rather than just quoted prices [-- reinvestment mechanics determine what you
actually accumulate](/articles/drips-automatically-reinvesting-dividends/). Holding bonds instead delivers contractual income
with different hazards: rate-driven price movements and issuer creditworthiness, without equity upside to compensate
[-- the fixed-income page covers the asymmetry](/articles/bonds-the-other-side-of-the-portfolio/). A laddered set of maturities
supplies scheduled cash without forecasting, and matters far more for money with deadlines than any equity income stream could.

Run your own check with invented round numbers to internalise the method rather than memorising thresholds. Suppose operations
generate roughly one hundred units annually while distributions total eighty and maintenance spending consumes sixty -- the
difference must come from asset sales, new borrowing or shrinking the business, none of which compounds indefinitely. Now compare
a firm generating similar cash with distributions near thirty: room exists for a poor year without policy change, which is the
actual definition of safety and has nothing whatsoever to do with advertised yield.

Finally, consider whether dividends deserve the reverence they receive. Total return comprises price change plus distributions,
and funds producing consistent payouts while assets decline have delivered negative experiences dressed as income. Tax treatment
varies by wrapper and jurisdiction, and inside taxable accounts repeated distributions create tracking obligations disproportionate
to the amounts involved. Decide what income stream you genuinely need at what horizon; if the honest answer involves money arriving
within a few years, equities of any stripe describe the wrong instrument entirely [-- allocation by deadline outranks security
selection](/articles/asset-allocation-balancing-stocks-and-bonds/), and pretending otherwise turns a planning question into a
stock-picking one, which almost always ends with someone owning something they cannot afford to hold through its bad quarter.

This is educational explanation of dividend analysis, not financial advice or a recommendation to buy or sell any security or
fund; all figures are invented illustrations supplied for checking reasoning, sector conventions shift with regulation and rates,
past payment behaviour neither guarantees continuation nor predicts it, investing involves risk, and dividends can be reduced or
eliminated at any time including after the longest unbroken record.
